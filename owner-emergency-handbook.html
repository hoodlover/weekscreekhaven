Emergency Handbook

Fast decisions for Lance, Heather, and the local response team when a guest stay goes sideways.

Life safety comes first. For fire, medical emergency, suspected gas or carbon monoxide, active crime, or immediate danger: tell the guest to leave the hazard area and call 911. Do not troubleshoot a life-threatening situation by text.

First five minutes

  1. Ask: “Is everyone safe right now?”
  2. Get the caller’s name, cabin location, callback number, and exact problem.
  3. For immediate danger, direct them to 911 and safe evacuation.
  4. Time-stamp the report and save photos/messages.
  5. Notify the local contact and decide who owns the response.

Lockout or access failure

  • Verify the primary guest and reservation dates.
  • Confirm the correct door and code instructions.
  • Issue a temporary code only through the approved lock system.
  • If unresolved in 15 minutes, dispatch the local contact or locksmith.
  • Never disclose a permanent owner code.

Power, water, heat, or internet

  • Determine whether the issue is property-wide or regional.
  • Check breakers, shutoffs, outage maps, and known reset instructions only when safe.
  • Dispatch the appropriate vendor when an essential service cannot be restored promptly.
  • Record guest impact and any written credit or refund decision.

Fire, smoke, gas, or CO alarm

  • Tell guests to evacuate immediately and call 911.
  • Do not instruct them to reenter or silence an unexplained alarm.
  • Notify the local contact and owners.
  • Do not permit reentry until emergency officials say it is safe.

Injury, creek, wildlife, or hot tub

  • For serious injury or uncertain severity, call 911.
  • Do not diagnose or promise reimbursement.
  • Preserve camera, message, maintenance, and witness records.
  • Photograph the area after responders release it and notify insurance when appropriate.

Severe weather or unsafe roads

  • Share official warnings and evacuation instructions.
  • Advise guests not to cross flooded roads or ignore closures.
  • Keep emergency access and the driveway clear.
  • If the property becomes uninhabitable, move to the relocation/refund decision below.

Noise, neighbor, or occupancy complaint

  • Verify the report and contact the primary guest once, clearly and calmly.
  • Require immediate correction and document the warning.
  • Dispatch the local contact for a verified continuing violation.
  • For threats, violence, illegal activity, or refusal to comply, contact law enforcement; do not attempt physical removal.

Property uninhabitable

  • Stop new access and protect guest safety.
  • Document the condition and expected repair time.
  • Owner decides: rapid repair, comparable relocation, shortened stay, or cancellation.
  • Confirm every financial decision in writing and process refunds through Square.
